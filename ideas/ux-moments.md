# Explore the experience before the data model
Status: exploring
Raised by: Thomas, 2026-09-18

## The idea
Design how people interact with all of this first. Once we know what the moments feel like, design the data model to serve them. A headless model with many small renderers is attractive, but only if it isn't confusing to build or to use, and we can't judge that in the abstract.

## How
Static, clickable mockups with made-up data. No backend, no accounts, cheap to throw away. One mockup per **moment**: a specific person in a specific place trying to do one thing. Candidates:

1. **A decision reaches me.** I'm in Discord. Something on Spearhead needs my input. What do I see, and can I act without leaving?
2. **I have an opinion while looking at the work.** I'm in the CAD viewer on a part, or looking at a board. I want to say something or see what's been said.
3. **We write a bounty spec together.** One document, suggested edits, inline comments, visible attribution.
4. **The lead decides.** Everything needed to make the call on one screen, including objections nobody answered.
5. **A newcomer asks "what's settled?"** Browsing the decision register for an aircraft. What's decided, what's open, what depends on what.
6. **Where the money goes.** A token holder looks at budgets, funding rounds, and who got paid for what.
7. **What changed since I last looked.** The catch-up view.

## Risks and tensions
Mockups flatter ideas. Each one should be tried on a real person doing a real task, and the awkward parts written down.

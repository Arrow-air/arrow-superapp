# Arrow superapp

The app: the Quiver and Longshot workspaces, live at [app.arrowair.com](https://app.arrowair.com). Sign in with the same GitHub or email account as flights.arrowair.com. The project switcher in the workspace header moves between them.

Agreed with Gavin on 2026-10-01 as the canonical version. It began as `prototypes/quiver-demo`, a fork of Gavin's `prototypes/app-frame` (branch `sl33ty/explore/app-frame` at `31d499a`); the frame, tokens, command palette, drawers and the thread design are his. His later app-frame work (merged to main as #13, through `d28575e`) is ported in too: the CAD explorer, thread types, the new-thread composer, option cards, and the design freeze clock. His Back this / poll-bar voting was tried and reverted (Thomas, 2026-10-01): voting stays Reddit-style arrows.

**What it's for:** anyone on Quiver can open the zone they care about (an attachment, QuiverHub, GPS and RF), see what exists for it, and weigh in on its open questions; calls can run off it; decisions land in a numbered register and can be funded as bounties or grants.

## How it works

- **Tabs are places, then views.** Overview, Attachments, Software, Build and Selling hold the working zones; after the divider, Discussion, Decisions and Work cut across all of them.
- **A zone is one page that scrolls:** what it is (and, for an attachment, its status, port, power and data), its discussion as rows, then what the call notes and GitHub already say about it.
- **A thread is a side panel, the same everywhere.** Rows in a zone, the Discussion index, the decision register, the Road to selling cards, call notes and People all open the same panel over the page (`?thread=Q-3`). Esc or ✕ closes it; ⤢ expands it. Nothing opens on its own.
- **Inside a thread, one order:** the question, then one Reddit-style comment tree with voting at every level. Every comment has Reddit-style up/down arrows with its weighted score. Top-level comments are the options: lettered A, B, C on their own cards; one can lead and a lead can adopt one. Replies nest under any option to any depth. The lead's decision block (a required note, an override warning) closes the thread.
- **Thread types** (Gavin's): questions grass, proposals teal, ideas amber, in rows, the thread header and the composer. Every place a thread starts uses one composer (`modules/threads/NewThread.vue`): a header strip that reads "New thread in *zone* about *part*", the title as the first line, the type chosen inside the box.
- **Design freeze clock** (Gavin's, `frame/freeze.ts`): counts down to the v1.1 freeze date a lead sets on the v1.1 page (end of that day, UTC), in the footer, the version menu, the model page and on each open v1.1 thread (grey, amber inside 14 days, red inside 48 hours). From the date, new threads go to v1.2; open ones still get settled, deferred or declined before the lead records the freeze. No date set, no clock.
- **Demo scaffolding lives in one footer menu:** view as member, core or lead; intent to build; reset.
- **Discussion → spec → funded work** (ported from `spec-threads`). A lead gives each thread an outcome: adopt a position into the spec (a D-number, a required note, an override warning), decline it with a reason, or defer it to Dev Kit v1.2. A decision can then be funded as a bounty (a fixed deliverable anyone claims) or a grant (scoped work someone takes on), with acceptance criteria and an ARROW reward. Work moves draft → open → claimed → in review → accepted. The adopted idea's author gets a 25% proposer award; when the notes are the only evidence of who raised it, the award is held until a lead confirms. The thread panel shows where each thread is: Discussion → Decided → Bounty/Grant. Work › Grants & bounties lists them all.
- **Retro pool and freeze** (DECISIONS 2026-09-23). A lead sets a retro pool and freeze date for v1.1. The pool splits across every position on a v1.1 thread by weighted net support, adopted or not (`modules/threads/retro.ts`, whole tokens, largest remainders); the v1.1 page previews it from the votes in the browser. Freezing needs every v1.1 thread settled (adopt, decline, or defer; "defer the rest" clears the way), then records the split and locks the spec. Nothing is paid from the app.
- **Dev Kit v1.1 and the 3D model.** Overview opens on the v1.1 improvements page: every thread aimed at the next revision, grouped by structure, GPS and RF, propulsion, power, avionics, and harness and wiring, with the decided change list. The 3D model (Overview › 3D model) is the Dev Kit assembly in Gavin's CAD explorer: a blueprint viewer (3D, top, front and side views; perspective or orthographic; grid; camera glides; right-drag pans) beside an inspector that drills down from the areas a change would be discussed in, to an area's parts, to one part, as tiles cut from the model. Layers switch assemblies off; Start a thread is pinned to the inspector's foot. The thread is anchored to the part's BOM number and lands in the zone that part belongs to (`src/data/model.ts`). The bill of materials shows open threads per part.

## Longshot

Longshot, Arrow's own battery pack, has its own workspace beside Quiver (added 2026-10-10 at Thomas's request): the whole project explained, with the same threads, votes, decisions, bounties, freeze and retro pool.

- **Tabs:** Overview (at a glance, 3D model, boards, people, call notes; PT2 improvements), Pack (cells, busbars, enclosure, mounting), Electronics (BMS, telemetry and CAN, charging, connector and sense boards), Aircraft (Quiver, Spearhead, testing), Build (bill of materials, building packs, suppliers and cost, safety, budget and proposal), then Discussion, Decisions and Work.
- **Every zone opens on what the record says**, a line per fact with its source: the repository and its issues, the proposal the DAO approved, the call notes on the Spearhead wiki (where Longshot's battery and BMS calls are recorded), Tattu's charger pages. Facts the sources disagree on show both (copper or brass busbars, polycarbonate or acrylic plates).
- **Threads L-1 to L-15** (`src/projects/longshot/threads.ts`) are seeded from those sources, with positions only where a source states them and people only as the notes name them. Longshot's integration into Quiver (fit, BMS protocol, failsafes, charging both packs, the first test) stays in Quiver's workspace as Q-18 to Q-22; Longshot's zones link to them under "Also discussed in Quiver".
- **3D model** (`public/longshot.glb`, 1 MB): the PT1 main assembly from the build123d model in project-longshot (PR #27), every part node named for its BOM part number; regenerate with `scripts/model/` (see its README).
- **Boards:** BMSJ in KiCanvas, the full BMS design imported from Julius's Vector BMS. Its schematic is complete; the layout hasn't started (only the connector is on the outline). BMSZ and the KiCad SL_PCB are basic layouts, so they're left out.
- **Bill of materials** from the build123d `BOM.csv`, and the known PT1 cost from `engineering/builds/PT1/BOM.md`. **Issues and pull requests** from GitHub, read-only.
- **Next version:** "Longshot PT2" (working name), with its own freeze clock and retro pool; the version after is "Longshot PT3". Version names differ between projects because the database keys freezes by version name.

How projects fit together: each project has its tabs (`src/frame/nav.ts`, `src/projects/longshot/nav.ts`) and its plan (`src/projects/plans.ts`: thread prefix, next version, next-version zones). Zone ids are unique across projects, so a thread's zone names its project; the frame keeps `currentProject` in step with the route, and the store's `projectThreads` is what each view lists. Part numbers and board ids never collide either.

## Live (app.arrowair.com)

With `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` set at build time, the app runs against the shared Arrow Supabase (the flight tracking app's), so people sign in with the same GitHub or email accounts. Reads are public; every write is a database function that checks who is signed in and, for lead actions, their role. Thomas and Erick are granted lead by GitHub account; leads set everyone else's role on the People page.

- Schema: `supabase/migrations/20261001000000_superapp_quiver.sql` (sa_-prefixed tables and functions only; nothing existing is touched).
- Who someone is (`20261024000000_sa_join_github_identity.sql`): `sa_join` takes the GitHub login, avatar and any granted role from `auth.identities`, which GoTrue writes from GitHub at sign-in, never from `user_metadata`, which every account can rewrite for itself (email sign-up is open on this project). A grant in `sa_role_grants` is made by login and pinned to GitHub's numeric id the first time it's used, so a renamed login that someone else registers gets nothing. The display name still falls back to the account's metadata. `supabase/tests/sa_join_identity_test.sql` checks all of this against a real database inside a transaction it rolls back.
- Sign in once across Arrow apps (`public/session-bridge.html`): flights.arrowair.com keeps its sign-in in this app's storage, through the bridge page in a hidden frame, so signing in or out on either app does both. The page answers only https://flights.arrowair.com (and, served over plain http on a developer's machine, pages on localhost or `*.arrowair.test`) and only Supabase's sign-in keys. No cookie is shared across arrowair.com: the forum and the website are hosted elsewhere. A storage change from flights reaches an open tab here as a `storage` event (`lib/session.ts`). The app marks its storage (`arrow-session-hub`, `src/main.ts`) whenever it opens as a page of its own, and the bridge tells flights whether it sees the mark: Safari and every iPhone browser give the hidden frame storage of its own, so flights keeps its own sign-in there and sign-in once works only in Chrome, Edge and Firefox. `public/serve.json` turns off serve's clean URLs, which otherwise sent `/session-bridge.html` to the app's catch-all page, and lets only flights.arrowair.com frame the bridge.
- Sign-in links use PKCE (`lib/backend.ts`): a link back from GitHub or an email signs in only the browser that asked for it, so nobody can send a link that signs someone into another account, which flights and the forum would then share. A confirmation link opened in another browser still confirms the address; they then sign in with their password. Password reset links come back here, and the sign-in box asks for the new password (`PASSWORD_RECOVERY`, `lib/session.ts`); from the forum's sign-in, it then carries on to the forum.
- Forum sign-in (`20261109000000_sa_forum_sign_in.sql`, `#/forum/sign-in`, `lib/forum.ts`): dao.arrowair.com signs people in with their Arrow account through DiscourseConnect. The forum sends the browser to `#/forum/sign-in?sso=…&sig=…`; once someone is signed in here, `sa_forum_sign_in` checks the forum's signature, then signs an answer naming the account (its id as `external_id`, its confirmed email, and GitHub login, name and avatar from `auth.identities`), and the browser goes back to the forum. The forum never sees the session. It refuses unconfirmed emails. The forum links a sign-in to an existing forum account (staff included) by email, and until 2026-11-09 sign-ups here were confirmed without any email being sent, so the answer says `require_activation=true` unless the address is proven (`20261110000000_sa_forum_proven_email.sql`): confirmed by a link this service emailed, the email GitHub verified on a GitHub sign-in, or vouched for in `sa_forum_vouched` by an admin who checked who owns it. Discourse then doesn't link by email; it makes a new forum account that the address's owner activates from their inbox, or refuses if the address is taken. The secret shared with the forum is Vault's `sa_forum_connect` (the forum's `discourse_connect_secret`). A sign-in that leaves the page (GitHub, an email confirmation) picks the forum request up again when it comes back, for 25 minutes; a request the database refuses is dropped. Signing in for the forum doesn't make someone a workspace member. Signing out or a ban here doesn't end a forum session: sign out there too, and for a ban also suspend or log out the forum account. The sign-in box can create an account and send a password reset link. `supabase/tests/sa_forum_sign_in_test.sql` checks the function inside a transaction it rolls back.
- Threaded comments (`20261001200000_sa_threaded_comments.sql`): comments live in `sa_positions` with `parent_id`; `sa_comment(thread, text, parent)` writes them; only top-level comments can be adopted. Existing replies were moved in (seeded ones under the option they answer).
- Reopening decisions (`20261001400000_sa_reopen_decision.sql`): a lead puts a decided or declined thread back into discussion ("Reopen as discussion" in the thread, optional reason). The outcome moves to the thread's history; votes and comments stay; draft or open work from it is withdrawn (kept, marked Withdrawn). Not once someone has claimed the work, not for decisions made outside the app, not in a frozen version. Decided again, the thread keeps its D-number.
- Drafts and flaky connections (`src/lib/drafts.ts`, `20261003000000_sa_retry_safe_writes.sql`): every composer keeps its text in the browser until the server confirms the save, so a dropped connection, a closed panel or a reload loses nothing; a failed save says why under the box and leaves the text there. Each draft carries a send key reused on every retry, so a save that landed but whose answer was lost isn't posted twice (`sa_comment` and `sa_start_thread` return the existing row for a repeated key).
- Editing threads (`20261001800000_sa_edit_thread.sql`): a thread's author or a lead edits its title and description in place (Edit in the thread's meta line); it shows "edited". Not once its version is frozen, since the frozen spec lists the titles. Earlier versions are kept in `sa_thread_edits` (no API access).
- Thread votes (`20261001700000_sa_thread_votes.sql`): up and down arrows on the thread itself, beside its title in the panel and on every thread row; weighted like comment votes; the same vote again takes it back; locked once the thread is decided or declined. `sa_vote_thread` is the only write.
- Editing comments (`20261001600000_sa_edit_comment.sql`): authors edit their own comments in place (Edit next to Reply); the comment shows "edited". Never the adopted option, which the decision quotes. Every earlier version is kept in `sa_comment_edits` (no API access), so what people voted on stays on record.
- Deleting bounties and grants (`20261001500000_sa_delete_work.sql`): leads only, while nobody has taken the work on (draft, open, or withdrawn after a reopen); "Delete bounty" on the work card, including withdrawn work shown on its thread. The row moves to `sa_deleted_work` (no API access); the decision can be funded again. W-numbers are not reused.
- Deleting comments (`20261001300000_sa_delete_comment.sql`): leads delete any comment or reply, anyone their own; the adopted option stays. Soft, Reddit style: the row keeps its place so replies under it stay (shown as "Deleted" or "Removed by a lead"), its words, author and source are cleared, and the original goes to `sa_deleted_comments`, which the API can't read. Votes on it stop counting; nothing new lands on it.
- Deleting threads (`20261001100000_sa_delete_thread.sql`): leads delete any thread, authors their own until someone else takes part, never once work is funded. Soft: the row keeps who, when and why, and the thread and everything on it disappear from reads.
- Projects (`20261010000000_sa_projects.sql`): `sa_projects` lists each project and its thread prefix (Q, L). `sa_start_thread` takes `p_project` (Quiver when a client names none) and numbers threads per project; `sa_settle` numbers decisions per project. Quiver keeps its counters (`thread`, `decision`); Longshot counts under `thread:longshot` and `decision:longshot`. Work stays W-1 upward across projects.
- Starting content: `npx tsx scripts/build-seed-sql.ts > supabase/seed_quiver.sql` and `npx tsx scripts/build-seed-sql.ts longshot > supabase/seed_longshot.sql` (idempotent).
- Applied to production by POSTing the SQL to the Supabase pg-meta endpoint (`/pg/query`, service key), after a `pg_dumpall` on the box.
- Hosting: Openship project `superapp-beta` (the project kept its old name), branch `main`, root `app/`: `npm run build` then `npm start` (serve on $PORT), always on. Every push to `main` redeploys. app.arrowair.com is the primary domain; superapp.arrowair.com, superapp-beta.arrowair.com and specs.arrowair.com are redirect domains on the same project (301, path and query kept; browsers keep the `#/…` route), so old links still land. The retired `spec-threads` project stays disabled rather than deleted: it shares this project's GitHub deploy hook, and deleting it would remove the hook.
- `e2e/live.cjs` runs the signed-in path against any Supabase with the migration (BASE, SUPA, ANON, SERVICE env).

## Registry

The contributor registry (`ideas/contributor-registry.md`): one list of everyone Arrow knows, with an account or not, what they said about themselves, and what the core team knows about them. Gavin's contributor map reads from it. Phase 1a is the database and the data layer (`src/lib/registry.ts`); phase 1b the newcomer's pages, `#/join` and `#/you`; phase 1c the Community workspace, the People list and each person's page.

- **What's stored** (`supabase/migrations/20261112000000_sa_registry.sql`): `sa_people`, a row per person (name, GitHub login and numeric id, Discord username and id, email, place, time zone, how much of the place the map shows (city, country or off, default off), where we know them from, the `ref` of the join link they came by, who added them, when they claimed the record); `sa_profiles`, what the person said (what they want to build, lanes, disciplines they can do and want to learn from taxonomy v1.0, experience, story, tools, what they'd build, travel, hours, when, what they want, what they never want to be asked, links, how they heard, a blurb, up to 3 disciplines for the map, whether they'd take an intro call and when, whether an agent drafted it). Team only: `sa_person_notes` (note, call, review, skill; earlier versions in `sa_person_note_edits`), `sa_outreach` (owner, status, next step, good for, the intro-call queue, its history; `say_hi` is there for the welcome-DM queue later), `sa_team_guesses` (disciplines and tools the team guesses for people who haven't said), `sa_skill_checks`, `sa_possible_matches`. All of it is keyed by person, so it stays when a record links to an account.
- **Who sees what.** Nothing reads these tables directly: RLS is on with no policies, and anon and authenticated have no grants (none of them is in an "sa public read" loop). Reads are functions. Anyone: `sa_registry_cards()`, the map's cards of people who have an account, finished their profile and chose the map, at their precision (country hides the city, lat and lng; lat and lng are rounded to 0.01°): name, place, time zone, up to 3 disciplines, the blurb, Discord and GitHub handles, the month they joined. Members (a row in `sa_members`): `sa_registry_people()` and `sa_registry_person(id)` give the full profiles of people with accounts, never emails, sources, call choices, people without accounts or anything the team wrote; `sa_my_profile()` is their own, call choices included. Core and leads: the same functions give everyone, with emails, sources, outreach, guesses, notes with their earlier versions, skill checks and open "same person?" cards (`sa_registry_matches()`).
- **Writes**, each a function that checks who's signed in: `sa_save_profile(fields, finish)` (your own, a step at a time; the first save makes your row; finishing needs what you want to build; the blurb is 140 characters with no links), `sa_add_person` (core or lead; name and source required; takes the GitHub or Discord id the app resolved, never trusts a handle alone, and refuses an id or email already on a record), `sa_update_person` (core or lead; once someone has an account only email, source and guesses, the rest is theirs), `sa_add_note` and `sa_edit_note` (core or lead; a note's author or a lead edits it), `sa_set_outreach` (core or lead), `sa_find_people` (core or lead; duplicates by name, Discord, GitHub or email), `sa_link_person` (leads settle a "same person?" card).
- **Linking** (`sa_registry_claim`, called by `lib/session.ts` right after `sa_join`; a failure only logs a warning): an unclaimed record links to the account whose GitHub or Discord numeric id it holds, from `auth.identities`, never `user_metadata` (Discord sign-in isn't enabled yet; the code is there). Then by the record's email, only when the account's email is proven by the forum sign-in's rule (`sa_forum_email_proven`: confirmed by a link we emailed, verified by GitHub, or vouched). A record whose ids name a different account is never linked by email. Anything weaker (the same name, a GitHub login or Discord username the team couldn't resolve to an id, an unproven email) gives the account a row of its own and a "same person?" card for a lead; so does a record the team adds later with an id or proven email of someone who already has a row. When a lead says same, the person's row and profile fold into the team's record, which keeps its notes and outreach; what they said wins over what the team typed, the ids the team resolved stay. A record, once claimed, never moves to another account.
- **Demo mode** keeps the mockups' sample people (`src/data/registry-sample.ts`) in this browser and applies the same rules for `registryDemo.role`, which follows the demo menu's View as (`src/modules/registry/community.ts`); the menu's Signed out (`setDemoSignedOut`, this tab only) makes the registry answer as for a visitor. The seed has one "same person?" card: an account named Rosa Delgado that may be the Rosa the team added. `resetRegistryDemo()` puts them back.
- **Join** (`#/join`, `src/pages/JoinPage.vue`; every way in links `/join?ref=…`, kept by `rememberJoinRef` until their record is made): one question a screen. Sign in (Discord shows as coming soon; GitHub and email through the existing sign-in), what they want to build in their own words and then the lanes ("Just following along" skips the four work questions), can do and want to learn (tap once, twice), the story, place with map precision, tools, would they build Arrow hardware and travel, time and time zone (the browser's by default), links and how they heard (filled in from the ref), and who sees what with a live preview of their public card. Answers are kept in this browser (`src/modules/registry/join.ts`) until saved; each step is saved with `saveProfile(fields, false)` and the last with `finish`. A GitHub or email sign-in that leaves the page comes back to `/join` at the next step (`rememberPendingJoin`, two hours, the forum's pattern; a waiting forum sign-in goes first). Someone with a finished profile edits it on the same steps. Database errors show next to their field.
- **Your Arrow** (`#/you`, the top bar's Contributor Dashboard; `/join` when signed out): the greeting and what they want to build, a getting-started list (a hello for #introductions drafted from their answers, with Copy and a link to Arrow's Discord; the welcome call once `WELCOME_CALL` in `src/modules/registry/you.ts` is set; one real thing), Matched for you (`src/modules/registry/matching.ts`: Quiver and Longshot zones and the Longshot manufacturing guide's steps, tagged with taxonomy disciplines, tools and lanes; open threads and unanswered guide questions, each saying why it matched), their public card, and their record (joined, how they came, threads, comments and guide answers).
- **Community** (`src/projects/community/nav.ts`, a project with no versions beside Quiver and Longshot in the switcher; routes in `src/router.ts`, pages in `src/pages/community/`). The sidebar (`communitySidebar` in `src/modules/registry/community.ts`, drawn by `SectionSidebar`) has the saved views with counts, the taxonomy groups as filters, and, for core and leads, Reach out. A tab with `team: true` shows to core and leads only, with a lock. Who is looking (`viewer`): signed out, a member, core or a lead, from the session (live) or the demo menu.
  - **People** (`#/community/people`, `PeoplePage.vue`): the view, taxonomy group, search and chips are in the query (`?view=new&cat=Engineering&q=lathe&shop=1&call=1`); the sidebar's links carry only the view, so picking one starts clean. Signed out: the public cards (`sa_registry_cards`) and search over them. Members: the table of everyone with an account (name, what they want to build, handle, place and their local time, can do and learning, tools, time), views All, New this week, Has a shop, and the Has a shop chip. Core and leads: everyone, people the team added marked "no account" with who added them and where from and the team's dotted guess at their skills; the Status column (status, open to a call, owner) and the note count; views Open to a call, Added by the team, Waiting on us 7+ days (finished a profile a week ago, status still new), Quiet 30+ days (marked quiet, or invited, booked or talked and nothing moved for 30 days), and the Open to a call chip. Has a shop is three or more tools; New this week is an account claimed (members: a profile finished) in the last 7 days. Leads see "same person?" cards above the list (`SameStrip.vue`; also on the record's page): Link them or Different people (`linkPerson`). No map view or "near a place" yet: nothing is geocoded (phase 2).
  - **A person** (`#/community/people/<id>`, `PersonPage.vue`): header (name, a No account pill and, for the team, the status; handles, place, local time and the difference from yours, how they came, what they want to build), Said (their story, their card's line, can do, learning, what fits, experience, wants, time, tools, would build, travel; the intro-call answer only when the API gave it), Linked (their links, GitHub, Discord; for the team, the email marked team only), Earned (threads, comments and guide answers in the app, as on Your Arrow: live by their account id, which members match through the GitHub login of a workspace member).
